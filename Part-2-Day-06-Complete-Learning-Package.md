#### 49. **exacerbate** *(verb)*
- **Simple English Definition:** To make a problem, bad situation, or harmful condition **worse or more severe**.
- **Master SAT Triad (`Memorize Together!`):** **Mitigate** (Day 2 #18 = lessen harm) & **Ameliorate** (Day 4 #39 = make better) $\longleftrightarrow$ **Exacerbate** (= **make worse!**).
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** worsen, aggravate, intensify, compound · **Ant:** *mitigate*, *ameliorate*, alleviate.
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"Draining peatlands for timber plantations **exacerbates** regional wildfire risk because dried sphagnum moss ignites readily and smolders underground for weeks."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** Drying out wet peat bogs makes wildfire danger *much worse* (*exacerbates* it).
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Confusing *exacerbate* with *exaggerate* ("overstate in speech"). Overstating a storm on TV *exaggerates* it; warm ocean water *exacerbates* (physically worsens) the storm!
- **Short Application Activity:** Choose `exaggerated` or `exacerbated`: *"Prolonged summer drought \_\_\_\_\_\_ bark-beetle tree mortality across Sierra Nevada pine forests."* (`exacerbated`).

#### 50. **unprecedented** *(adjective)*
- **Simple English Definition:** Never done, seen, or experienced before; having **zero earlier historical example**.
- **Root Clue:** `un-` (*not*) + `precede` (*come before*) = **"nothing like this ever came before!"**
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** unmatched, novel, unparalleled, unheard-of · **Ant:** *conventional* (Day 2 #15), routine, customary, familiar.
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"Ice-core bubbles spanning eight hundred thousand years show that modern atmospheric carbon dioxide concentrations have risen at an **unprecedented** pace since industrialization."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** Across $800{,}000$ years of trapped air, no earlier period ever matched today's speed of increase (*unprecedented*).
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Confusing *unprecedented* with *unpredictable* ("hard to forecast") or *unimportant*!
- **Short Application Activity:** If a museum exhibition draws **unprecedented** attendance, what happened? (`More visitors came than ever before in the museum's history!`)

#### 51. **impede** *(verb)* / **impediment** *(noun)*
- **Simple English Definition:** To slow down, obstruct, or get in the way of movement or progress.
- **Degree Precision (`impede` vs. `preclude`):** **Impede** = *slow down / hinder* (progress is harder, though maybe still possible); **Preclude** (Day 4 #33) = *make 100% impossible*!
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** hinder, obstruct, hamper, retard · **Ant:** facilitate, *catalyze* (Day 5 #42), expedite.
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"Low-head nineteenth-century mill dams **impede** upstream Atlantic shad migrations without completely blocking floods during spring high water."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** Notice *"without completely blocking"*—the dams *obstruct and slow down* (*impede*) fish passage rather than making it 100% impossible.
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Confusing *impede* with *stampede* or *speed*. Remember Latin `ped` = foot (`im-pede` = tangle someone's feet so they walk slowly!).
- **Short Application Activity:** Scale from partial hindrance (`1`) to total impossibility (`2`): *preclude*, *impede*. (`1 = impede`, `2 = preclude`).

#### 52. **vindicate** *(verb)*
- **Simple English Definition:** To prove that someone—or a doubted theory—was **right and justified all along** after earlier criticism or disbelief.
- **Classic SAT Passage Arc:** `Scientist X was mocked in 1920 -> 1960s data proved Scientist X right` = **"vindicated Scientist X!"**
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** justify, validate, exonerate, bear out · **Ant:** *refute* (Day 3 #25), discredit, disprove.
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"Decades after geologists dismissed Alfred Wegener's continental-drift hypothesis, mid-ocean ridge paleomagnetic stripes **vindicated** his core insight that continents move horizontally."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** Seafloor magnetic data *proved Wegener right after years of dismissal* (*vindicated* him).
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Confusing **vindicate** ("prove right / clear of doubt" — positive!) with **vindictive** ("spiteful / seeking revenge" — negative!)! Watch the ending carefully: **`vindicate` (`+`)** vs. **`vindictive` (`-`)**!
- **Short Application Activity:** Did Barbara McClintock's Nobel Prize for "jumping genes" after decades of skepticism **vindicate** or **repudiate** her early maize experiments? (`Vindicate!`)

#### 53. **discrepancy** *(noun)*
- **Simple English Definition:** An unexpected mismatch or difference between two numbers, reports, or facts that ought to agree.
- **Cross-Section Utility:** Appears constantly in SAT science anomalies (*"account for the discrepancy between predicted and observed orbits"*) and historical source comparisons.
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** mismatch, inconsistency, divergence, disparity · **Ant:** agreement, concordance, consistency.
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"A persistent two-percent **discrepancy** between customs export ledgers in Mocha and import manifests in Marseille alerted historians to widespread Mediterranean coffee smuggling."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** Export numbers and import numbers *failed to match* (*discrepancy*), revealing off-the-books smuggling.
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Confusing *discrepancy* with *discretion* ("careful judgment/privacy").
- **Short Application Activity:** Connect Day 1 + Day 6: When scientists find a **discrepancy** between two datasets, they design experiments to **\_\_\_\_\_\_** (Day 1 #7: bring into harmony) them! (`reconcile`).

#### 54. **feasible** *(adjective)* / **feasibility** *(noun)*
- **Simple English Definition:** Possible, workable, and realistic to carry out practically with available time, money, and technology.
- **Difference from `plausible` (Day 1 #4):** **Plausible** = *believable as a true explanation/hypothesis*; **Feasible** = *practically doable as an engineering/policy project*!
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** workable, viable, practicable, achievable · **Ant:** unfeasible, impractical, unworkable, prohibitive.
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"Advances in directional drilling made subsea power cables across deep Norwegian fjords economically **feasible** for the first time in the 1990s."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** Better drilling technology made underwater fjord cables *practically and financially doable* (*feasible*).
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Mixing up *plausible* (truth of an explanation) vs. *feasible* (practicality of an action). You don't call a fossil theory "feasible" or a bridge construction budget "plausible"!
- **Short Application Activity:** Pick `plausible` or `feasible`: *"Solar desalination proved technically \_\_\_\_\_\_ on the arid island, offering a \_\_\_\_\_\_ explanation for why villagers stayed."* (`feasible` = doable project / `plausible` = believable explanation!).

#### Detailed Distractor-by-Distractor Teaching Explanations

1. **`RW-D6-Q1` — Correct Answer: B (`stools`)**
   - **Clause-Stripping Analysis:** `[Designed by Finnish architect Alvar Aalto in 1933,]` = introductory participial modifier. Core clause: **Subject** = *three-legged bent-birch stools* $\to$ **Finite Verb** = *stack*. Standard English forbids placing a single comma (**A**), semicolon (**C** — left side lacks a finite verb!), or single dash (**D**) directly between a subject and its verb.

2. **`RW-D6-Q2` — Correct Answer: B (`consequently,`)**
   - **Logical Relationship:** Sentence 1 explains why fresh pineapple ruins gelatin (active bromelain snips collagen). Sentence 2 Clause 1 states that canning heat above $80^\circ\text{C}$ denatures bromelain completely (**Cause**) $\to$ Clause 2 states that cooks can safely fold canned pineapple into gelatin molds (**Effect**). **B (`consequently,`)** signals cause-and-effect accurately.
   - **Why Distractors Fail:** **A (`however,`)** and **C (`conversely,`)** signal contrast between two clauses that actually agree in cause-effect; **D (`for instance,`)** introduces a specific example rather than a causal result.

3. **`RW-D6-Q3` — Correct Answer: B (`catalyzes`)**
   - **5-Step Context Prediction:** *"Far from treating damaged coral reefs as beyond recovery"* establishes optimistic, positive restoration polarity: stabilizing shifting rubble *sparks/accelerates* natural regrowth (**B: `catalyzes`**).
   - **Why Distractors Fail:** **A (`impedes` = slows)**, **C (`precludes` = makes impossible)**, and **D (`repudiates` = rejects)** all reverse the sentence's positive restoration meaning.

4. **`RW-D6-Q4` — Correct Answer: B (`ostensibly`)**
   - **5-Step Context Prediction:** `Although` signals a surface-vs.-reality contradiction: canal charters *on paper / supposedly* guaranteed free passage to village skiffs, yet lockkeepers filled every daylight hour with timber barges so villagers couldn't actually use the canal for free. **B (`ostensibly`)** means "outwardly/supposedly on the surface, masking an opposite practical reality."
   - **Why Distractors Fail:** **A (`feasibly`)** means practicably doable; **C (`stringently`)** means strictly enforced (if free skiff passage had been *stringently* guaranteed, lockkeepers could not have blocked villagers all day!); **D (`empirically`)** means by experimental observation.

5. **`RW-D6-Q5` — Correct Answer: B (`vindicated`)**
   - **5-Step Context Prediction:** In $1869$, senior biochemists dismissed Miescher's "nuclein" as mere protein contamination; 75 years later, Avery's experiments *proved Miescher right after decades of dismissal* (**B: `vindicated`**).
   - **Why Distractors Fail:** **A (`exacerbated`)** = worsened a problem; **C (`mitigated`)** = softened harm; **D (`circumscribed`)** = drew restrictive limits around.

6. **`RW-D6-Q6` — Correct Answer: B**

     - `T1:` `+ nocturnal cargo trams (zero tunnels, less double-parking)`
     - `T2:` `± concedes congestion/tunneling perks, BUT >68 dB curve squeal requires rail dampers + lubrication first`

7. **`RW-D6-Q7` — Correct Answer: C**

     1. Open-bottom gravel arches restore temperature, oxygen, and trout passage *nearly to unbridged natural levels* ($16.5^\circ\text{C}$ vs. $16.2^\circ\text{C}$, $9.4$ vs. $9.6\text{ mg/L}$, $89\%$ vs. $92\%$).

8. **`RW-D6-Q8` — Correct Answer: B**

1. **`MATH-D6-Q1` — Correct Answer: C (`16`)**

2. **`MATH-D6-Q2` — Correct Answer: `280` (SPR)**

3. **`MATH-D6-Q3` — Correct Answer: A ($\dfrac{7}{25}$)**
   - **5-Second Complementary Trig Identity:** Because $\angle L = 90^\circ$ in right $\triangle JKL$, acute angles $J$ and $K$ satisfy $J + K = 90^\circ$. The side adjacent to $\angle J$ is the exact same leg opposite $\angle K$, so $\sin K = \cos J = \dfrac{7}{25}$ (**A**) with zero Pythagorean calculation needed!
   - **Trap Alert:** Choice **B ($\dfrac{24}{25}$)** wastes 30 seconds finding the other leg ($24$) and accidentally computes $\cos K$!

4. **`MATH-D6-Q4` — Correct Answer: `4` (SPR)**
   - **Simplify Left Side:** $\dfrac{1}{2}(8x - 10) = 4x - 5$.
   - **Apply No-Solution Rule (`Day 2 Lesson 2.4`):** A linear equation $4x - 5 = kx + 3$ has **no solution** when the $x$-coefficients (slopes) are identical ($k = 4$) and the constants ($y$-intercepts) differ ($-5 \neq 3$). Check $k = 4$: $4x - 5 = 4x + 3 \implies -5 = 3$ (never true). Type **`4`**.

5. **`MATH-D6-Q5` — Correct Answer: B (`-7`)**
   - **5-Second Vieta Shortcut:** For $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ ($a = 5, b = 35, c = -18$):
     1. Confirm real roots exist: $D = 35^2 - 4(5)(-18) = 1{,}225 + 360 = 1{,}585 > 0$ (two distinct real solutions).
     2. Sum of solutions $= -\dfrac{b}{a} = -\dfrac{35}{5} = -7$ (**B**).
   - **Trap Alert:** **A (`-35`)** forgets to divide by $a = 5$; **C (`7`)** drops the negative sign in $-b/a$.

6. **`MATH-D6-Q6` — Correct Answer: C**
   - **Exponential Anatomy:** In $B(t) = 1{,}250(1.06)^t$, $1{,}250$ is the initial deposit at $t = 0$, and $1.06 = 1 + 0.06$ is the **annual growth factor**: each year, the balance is multiplied by $1.06$ (**C**), which equals a $6\%$ annual increase.
   - **Why Distractors Fail:** **A** treats an exponential base like a linear slope ($\$1.06$/year); **B** confuses a $1.06$ multiplier ($+6\%$) with $+106\%$ (which would be a $2.06$ multiplier!); **D** confuses base with doubling time.

7. **`MATH-D6-Q7` — Correct Answer: B ($\dfrac{13}{22}$)**
   - **Conditional Denominator Firewall (`Day 3 Lesson 3.5`):** Underline *"selected at random from **those who use a monthly pass**."* That phrase locks your denominator to the **Monthly Pass column total = `110`**!
   - **Numerator:** Commuters in that column who ride **Regional Rail = `65`**.
   - **Simplify:** $\dfrac{65}{110} = \dfrac{65 \div 5}{110 \div 5} = \dfrac{13}{22}$ (**B**).
   - **Trap Alert:** **A ($\dfrac{65}{180} = \dfrac{13}{36}$)** uses the grand total `180`; **C ($\dfrac{65}{100} = \dfrac{13}{20}$)** divides by the Regional Rail row total `100` instead of the Monthly Pass column total!

8. **`MATH-D6-Q8` — Correct Answer: `5` (SPR)**
   - **Desmos Method (`12 Seconds` — Zero Sign Risk!):**
     1. Line 1: `y = (x - 2)^2 - 4`
     2. Line 2: `y = 2x - 5`
     3. Click both intersection dots: $(1, -3)$ and $(5, 5)$. Since $x_1 > x_2$, $x_1 = 5$. Type **`5`**.
   - **Algebra Backup:** $(x - 2)^2 - 4 = 2x - 5 \implies (x^2 - 4x + 4) - 4 = 2x - 5 \implies x^2 - 6x + 5 = 0 \implies (x - 5)(x - 1) = 0 \implies x = 5 \text{ or } 1 \implies x_1 = 5$.

9. **`MATH-D6-Q9` — Correct Answer: C ($25\pi$)**
   - **Step 1 (Box Target):** Box **`area`** ($\pi r^2$, NOT radius $r$!).
   - **Step 2 (Complete the Square):** Move $+9$ to the right side and add $(6/2)^2 = 9$ and $(-10/2)^2 = 25$ to both sides:
     $$(x^2 + 6x + 9) + (y^2 - 10y + 25) = -9 + 9 + 25$$
     $$(x + 3)^2 + (y - 5)^2 = 25$$
   - **Step 3:** Here $r^2 = 25$, so $\text{Area} = \pi r^2 = 25\pi$ (**C**).
   - **Trap Alert:** **A ($5\pi$)** plugs in $r = 5$ instead of $r^2 = 25$; **B ($10\pi$)** finds circumference $2\pi r$; **D ($43\pi$)** forgets to flip the sign of $+9$ when moving it across the equals sign ($+9 + 9 + 25 = 43$).

10. **`MATH-D6-Q10` — Correct Answer: D (`8`)**
    - **Discriminant Tangency Rule (`Day 4 Lesson 4.4`):** A quadratic $kx^2 - 8x + 2 = 0$ ($k \neq 0$) has **exactly one real solution** if and only if its discriminant equals zero:
      $$b^2 - 4ac = (-8)^2 - 4(k)(2) = 64 - 8k = 0 \implies 8k = 64 \implies k = 8 \text{ (D)}$$
