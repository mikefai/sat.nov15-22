# Personalized Digital SAT Vocabulary Notebook & Spaced-Review System

> **Core Principle for International Students:** SAT *Words in Context* and reading passages do **not** reward memorizing long lists of rare English words or translating word-for-word into your first language. They reward **contextual precision**: understanding how academic verbs, tone adjectives, and logical connectors shape an author's claim.

---

## 1. How to Use This Notebook Daily

1. **Daily Selection Target:** Record **8–12 high-value academic words or expressions** per day discovered in official SAT practice, your Error Log, or your daily workbook lessons (aim for **8 words** on full-test or heavy passage-analysis days so quality stays high; **40–60 words** mastered deeply by Day 7).
2. **Write Definitions in Simple English:** Explain the word as if teaching a younger student in clear, everyday English. Use first-language (L1) translation only for a 5-second check if an abstract concept is still unclear—then immediately write and practise in English.
3. **Record the Logical Clue:** Underline the contrast, cause-and-effect, colon, or parallel structure in the SAT sentence that proves the word's meaning.
4. **Complete Spaced Retrieval Without Looking:** Cover Columns 2–6 when reviewing on scheduled dates. Test whether you can:
   - Recognize the word immediately in print.
   - Explain its meaning in simple English.
   - Identify its contextual meaning (especially secondary academic meanings, such as *qualify* = "limit a claim," not "meet requirements").
   - Spot logical clues around it.
   - Distinguish it from close synonyms or trap distractors.
   - Apply it accurately in a brand-new sentence or passage.

---

## 2. High-Utility Academic Word Parts (Keep Morphology Practical)

Do **not** memorize dozens of obscure Latin/Greek roots. Focus only on directional prefixes and part-of-speech suffixes that help you judge **positive/negative polarity** and **grammatical role** under time pressure:

| Word Part | Core Signal | Academic SAT Examples | How It Helps Under Time Pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **sub-** | under, beneath, supporting from below | *substantiate*, *subordinate*, *subtle* | Signals either foundational support (*substantiate*) or lower rank (*subordinate*). |
| **cor- / con- / com-** | together, with | *corroborate*, *concur*, *compelling*, *reconcile* | Signals agreement, alignment, or bringing ideas together. |
| **ambi- / equiv-** | both ways, equal weight | *ambiguous*, *ambivalent*, *equivocal* | Signals **uncertainty, mixed feelings, or two possible meanings** (never extreme certainty). |
| **un- / in- / im- / ir- / dis-** | not, opposite of | *undermine*, *implausible*, *indifferent*, *disparate* | Flips sentence polarity immediately—watch for double negatives (*not implausible* = reasonable). |
| **re-** | back, again | *reconcile*, *refute*, *resilient*, *retract* | Signals restoring harmony (*reconcile*) or pushing back against a claim (*refute*). |
| **-ate / -ify / -ize** | verb ("to make or do") | *substantiate*, *scrutinize*, *corroborate* | Tells you the blank requires an action/relationship between subject and object. |
| **-ous / -ive / -able / -ible** | adjective ("having the quality of") | *ambiguous*, *tentative*, *plausible*, *compelling* | Tells you the word modifies a noun's tone, strength, or certainty. |
| **-tion / -ity / -ance / -ence** | abstract noun | *substantiation*, *ambiguity*, *plausibility* | Helps convert a familiar verb/adjective into a noun without treating it as a brand-new word. |
