### Lesson 7.2: The 7-Step Vocabulary Error Repair Protocol & Master R&W Firewalls

Whenever `E2` (`V1`, `V2`, `V4`, or `V5`) contributed to a Practice Test 5 miss or `[?]` guess, run all **7 Repair Steps** (`3 minutes per word` — far more effective than staring at flashcards!):

1. **Step 1 — Isolate the Exact Blocking Word:** Was it in the passage, the question stem (*substantiate, undermine, qualify*), or the answer choices?
2. **Step 2 — Write a 5-Word Simple English Meaning:** Strip dictionary jargon down to everyday English (*e.g., circumscribe = draw strict limits around*).
3. **Step 3 — Re-Read the Original SAT Context & Circle the Logical Clue:** Find the exact colon, semicolon, `although`, `far from`, or parallel verb that *forced* that meaning—even if you hadn't known the word!
4. **Step 4 — Map Polarity (`+ / - / ±`) + 2 Synonyms & 1 Contrast:** Anchor the word inside an existing cluster (*e.g., mitigate/ameliorate `[+]` vs. exacerbate `[-]`*).
5. **Step 5 — Guard Against Lookalike / Polysemy Traps:** Note any treacherous twin (*circumscribe* = restrict limits $\neq$ *circumvent* = bypass; *underscore* = emphasize $\neq$ *undermine* = weaken; *afford* = provide/supply $\neq$ buy).
6. **Step 6 — Write One Original Sentence About Science, History, or Daily Life:** Active generation cements neural retrieval (`3x` stronger than silent reading).
7. **Step 7 — Schedule 48-Hour & 7-Day Spaced Retrieval Dates:** Enter `Nov 22` and `Nov 27` in Column 7 of [Vocabulary-Notebook-and-Spaced-Review.md](./Trackers/Vocabulary-Notebook-and-Spaced-Review.md).

#### Quick-Reference Master R&W Firewalls (Review Before `RW-D7-Q1`–`Q12`)
- **Firewall 1 (`SEC Boundaries`):** `IC , IC` is **ALWAYS WRONG** (even with `, however,` or `, therefore,`!). Only `.`, `;`, `:`, `—`, or `, + FANBOYS` can legally join two independent clauses.
- **Firewall 2 (`SEC Finite Verb vs. Modifier`):** Cross out `[prepositional phrases]`, `[participial -ing/-ed phrases]`, and `[who/which/that clauses]`. Every main clause MUST have at least one **finite tense-bearing verb** (`is/was/have/writes/wrote` — NEVER bare `writing` or `to write`!).
- **Firewall 3 (`SEC Parallel Comparisons & Introductory Modifiers`):** Check singular **`that of`** vs. plural **`those of`** against the exact compared noun; and verify that the noun immediately after `[Introductory -ing/-ed phrase],` is the actual doer of that action!
- **Firewall 4 (`Polysemy Alert`):** Common English words often wear academic disguises on Hard SAT items: ***qualify*** ("limit/add conditions to"), ***check*** ("restrain/slow"), ***arrest*** ("stop/halt"), ***yield*** ("produce" or "surrender"), ***afford*** ("provide/offer access to"), ***weather*** ("endure/survive safely").

#### 55. **circumscribe** *(verb)*
- **Simple English Definition:** To draw strict boundaries around something; to **restrict or limit** power, freedom, or scope.
- **Root Clue & Lookalike Firewall:** `circum-` (*around*) + `scribe` (*write/draw a line*) = **draw a limiting circle around**! **NEVER confuse with `circumvent` (Day 4 #32 = `circum` + `vent` [go around / bypass an obstacle])!**
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** restrict, confine, bound, limit · **Ant:** expand, broaden, liberate.
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"Venice's Great Council enacted thirteenth-century constitutional statutes specifically designed to **circumscribe** the Doge's personal treaty-making authority."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** The council passed laws to *tightly limit and bound* (*circumscribe*) what the ruler could decide alone.
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Mixing up *circumscribe* ("impose limits on") and *circumvent* ("cleverly get around limits")—almost exact opposites in institutional passages!
- **Short Application Activity:** Choose `circumscribed` or `circumvented`: *"Strict frost-free growing seasons \_\_\_\_\_\_ highland maize cultivation to sheltered valley floors below $2{,}800\text{ meters}$."* (`circumscribed`).

#### 56. **contentious** *(adjective)*
- **Simple English Definition:** Causing heated disagreement, debate, or controversy among scholars or the public.
- **Lookalike Firewall:** Related to the verb *contend* ("argue/struggle"), **NOT** the everyday adjective *content* ("happy/satisfied")!
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** controversial, disputed, debated, polarizing · **Ant:** uncontroversial, settled, consensus, undisputed.
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"Whether Clovis stone points represent the earliest human presence in North America—once textbook orthodoxy—became deeply **contentious** after Monte Verde radiocarbon dates preceded Clovis by a millennium."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** Older archaeological dates turned a settled textbook belief into a *fiercely debated* (*contentious*) question.
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Seeing `content-` and guessing "peaceful/satisfied" (`+`), which completely flips the word's true debate/conflict meaning (`± / -`)!
- **Short Application Activity:** If two historians hold **contentious** views on a treaty, do they agree peacefully or argue sharply? (`Argue sharply / disagree!`)

#### 57. **subordinate** *(adjective / verb)*
- **Simple English Definition:** *(adj.)* Lower in rank, priority, or importance; *(verb)* to treat something as secondary to a more important goal.
- **Root Clue:** `sub-` (*under*) + `ord` (*order/rank*) = **ranked underneath**!
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** secondary, lesser, ancillary, subsidiary · **Con:** primary, paramount, *salient* (Day 5 #44), overarching.
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"In Bauhaus industrial workshops, decorative surface ornament was deliberately **subordinated** to structural durability and clean geometric function."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** Surface decoration was *treated as secondary* (*subordinated*) compared to strength and function.
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Knowing *subordinate clause* in grammar but missing the verb *"subordinate X to Y"* ("make X less important than Y") in art/policy passages.
- **Short Application Activity:** In exam-day Math Module 1, should speed be **subordinated** to accuracy on `Q1–Q15`, or accuracy subordinated to speed? (`Speed subordinated to accuracy—protect those routing points!`)

#### 58. **immutable** *(adjective)*
- **Simple English Definition:** Unchanging over time; fixed, permanent, and impossible to alter.
- **Root Clue:** `im-` (*not*) + `mut` (*change*, as in *mutation*) + `-able` = **unable to change**!
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** fixed, unchangeable, invariant, permanent · **Ant:** *ephemeral* (Day 5 #41), malleable, mutable, *tentative* (Day 1 #9).
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"Before Carl Woese's ribosomal RNA phylogeny, many microbiologists treated bacterial species boundaries as nearly **immutable** categories rather than fluid lineages shaped by horizontal gene transfer."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** Scientists once viewed bacterial species lines as *permanently fixed* (*immutable*), before discovering genes jump across species.
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Confusing *immutable* ("unchangeable") with *mute* ("silent")!
- **Short Application Activity:** Does modern neuroscience view adult brain wiring as **immutable** or adaptable (plastic)? (`Adaptable—NOT immutable!`)

#### 59. **conflate** *(verb)* / **conflation** *(noun)*
- **Simple English Definition:** To mistakenly blend, mix, or fuse two distinct ideas, measurements, or causes as if they were the exact same thing.
- **Master SAT Contrast Pair:** **Conflate** (*blur/lump two different things together — error!*) $\longleftrightarrow$ **Delineate** (Day 3 #27) & **Discern** (Day 5 #46) (*clearly separate and distinguish!*).
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** confuse, fuse, lump together, equate falsely · **Ant:** distinguish, differentiate, *delineate*, separate.
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"Public-health demographers caution against **conflating** crude hospital admission counts with true disease incidence, since expanded clinic access raises admissions even when infection rates fall."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** Analysts warn against *falsely treating two distinct metrics as identical* (*conflating* hospital visits with actual new infections).
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Guessing *conflate* means "inflate/exaggerate" because of the `-flate` ending (`con-flate` = blow two separate balloons into one!).
- **Short Application Activity:** If a student treats **correlation** (Day 5 #45) and **causation** as identical, which verb describes their mistake? (`They conflated correlation with causation!`)

#### 60. **analogous** *(adjective)*
- **Simple English Definition:** Comparable or parallel in structure, function, or logic despite belonging to different fields or species.
- **Master SAT Contrast Pair:** **Analogous** (*structurally parallel / comparable*) $\longleftrightarrow$ **Disparate** (Day 2 #16 = *fundamentally different and unrelated*).
- **Synonym / Antonym:** **Syn:** comparable, parallel, akin, corresponding · **Ant:** *disparate*, dissimilar, divergent, unrelated.
- **Original SAT-Style Contextual Sentence:** *"Termite-mound ventilation shafts perform a role **analogous** to mammalian lungs, harnessing wind-driven pressure gradients to exchange underground carbon dioxide for fresh oxygen."*
- **Explanation of Contextual Meaning:** Mud ventilation shafts and animal lungs look totally different, yet perform a *functionally parallel* (*analogous*) gas-exchange job.
- **Common International-Student Misunderstanding:** Confusing *analogous* ("comparable/parallel") with *ambiguous* ("unclear") or *anomalous* ("abnormal/outlier")! Watch that trio: **`analogous` (parallel)** vs. **`ambiguous` (unclear)** vs. **`anomalous` (unusual outlier)**.
- **Short Application Activity:** Are camera lenses and human eye lenses **analogous** or **disparate** optical structures? (`Analogous!`)
