## 3. Special Academic Vocabulary System for International Students

### A. What Vocabulary Actually Appears on the Digital SAT

Do **not** download archaic 1990s SAT word lists (*obsequious, defenestrate, lugubrious*). Digital SAT passages are 25–150-word academic excerpts drawn from peer-reviewed science, economics, sociology, history, and literary prose. High-return vocabulary falls into **six functional groups**:

1. **Argument & Evidence Verbs:** *substantiate, corroborate, undermine, bolster, refute, assert, posit, concede, reconcile, extrapolate, scrutinize, entail.*
2. **Epistemic Certainty & Caution Modifiers:** *plausible, tentative, speculative, definitive, inconclusive, compelling, dubious, empirical.*
3. **Nuance, Clarity & Complexity Adjectives:** *ambiguous, nuanced, overt, subtle, disparate, homogeneous, conventional, novel, ubiquitous.*
4. **Polysemous Words (Everyday Words with Secondary Academic Meanings):** *qualify* ("limit/moderate a claim"), *check* ("restrain/slow down"), *arrest* ("halt"), *observe* ("follow a rule" or "remark"), *appreciate* ("understand fully" or "increase in value"), *afford* ("provide"), *compromise* ("weaken/endanger").
5. **Logical Pivot & Concession Connectors:** *nevertheless, notwithstanding, albeit, conversely, consequently, moreover, inasmuch as, hitherto, contingent on.*
6. **Author Attitude & Rhetorical Purpose Verbs:** *critique, lament, underscore, qualify, contextualize, repudiate, commemorate, vindicate.*

---

### B. Three-Layer Vocabulary Instruction Model

Every new word studied during this week is learned through **three progressive layers**—never bare translation:

#### Model Demonstration: **Reconcile** *(verb)*
- **Layer 1 — Simple English Meaning:**  
  To find a way for two ideas, facts, or groups that seem to disagree to fit together without contradiction.
- **Layer 2 — SAT Academic Context:**  
  *"Paleoclimatologists struggled for decades to **reconcile** geological evidence of tropical glaciers with computer models predicting high carbon dioxide levels during the same era."*  
  **How it works in the sentence:** Notice the tension between *tropical glaciers* (extreme cold) and *high carbon dioxide* (warming). *Reconcile* expresses the scientists' effort to make those two conflicting facts fit into a single coherent explanation.
- **Layer 3 — Independent Application:**
  1. *Contextual MCQ Check:* In the sentence *"To ______ the museum's mission of public access with the fragility of ninth-century silk scrolls, curators display high-resolution replicas alongside rotating originals,"* why does **reconcile** work while **substantiate** fails? *(Because the curators are balancing two competing goals—access vs. preservation—not proving a claim true.)*
  2. *Productive Output:* Write one original sentence in [Vocabulary-Notebook-and-Spaced-Review.md](./Trackers/Vocabulary-Notebook-and-Spaced-Review.md) connecting two competing priorities in your own life or academic studies.
  3. *Transfer Check:* Recognize the negative adjective **irreconcilable** (*"irreconcilable differences between the two datasets"*) three days later without hints.

### C. Repeatable 5-Step Context-Clue Method (Use on Every *Words in Context* Question)

1. **Identify the Passage's Core Claim:** Strip away adjectives and side clauses—who or what is the main subject, and what is the central point?
2. **Spot the Logical Signal:** Circle punctuation and pivot markers:
   - **Colon (`:`) or Em-Dash (`—`):** Restates, defines, or illustrates the exact same idea (`Clause A = Clause B`).
   - **Support / Continuation (*moreover, indeed, similarly, consequently, thus*):** Blank matches the direction/tone of the adjacent evidence (`+ → +` or `- → -`).
   - **Contrast / Concession (*although, nevertheless, notwithstanding, whereas, yet, instead of, far from*):** Blank flips the direction/tone (`+ → -` or `- → +`).
3. **Predict a Simple Plain-English Word First:** Cover choices A–D with your hand or cursor and put a simple English phrase in the blank (e.g., *"weaken," "prove," "unclear," "temporary"*).
4. **Test All Four Choices Against Context (Never Familiarity):**
   - Do **not** pick a word just because you know it if it misses your prediction.
   - Do **not** eliminate an unfamiliar word if the three words you *do* know clearly contradict your prediction!
5. **Verify Author Meaning & Tone Precision:** Re-read the sentence with your chosen word inserted. Confirm that both **logical direction** and **degree of certainty** match (e.g., *tentative* fits cautious early data; *irrefutable* is too extreme).

---

### D. Language-Accessibility Rules for International Learners

- **Clause Stripping Before Translating:** Long English academic sentences hide simple cores inside participial phrases and appositives. Train your eye to bracket Everything between paired commas or dashes first:  
  `The hypothesis [proposed by early marine biologists after brief coastal surveys] was undermined by deep-ocean sonar.` → Core: **`The hypothesis was undermined by deep-ocean sonar.`**
- **Strategic First-Language (L1) Rule:** Never translate whole passages into your native language during practice—translation cuts reading speed in half and breaks English syntax intuition. During **untimed review only**, if an abstract English concept (like *concession* or *empirical*) feels fuzzy after reading the simple English definition, check your L1 equivalent for 5 seconds, then close the dictionary and write your example sentence in English.
- **Zero Simplification During Timed Sets:** Timed practice always uses authentic SAT sentence complexity; scaffolding happens strictly during instruction and post-set analysis.

## 5. High-Return Score-Improvement Strategy (1200 → 1400+)

### A. Reading & Writing: Climbing from 560 toward 650–700+

For an international student at **560 R&W**, trying to "read faster and memorize 1,000 words" is the slowest path to points. Instead, attack R&W in **order of rule predictability and speed return** (subject to Day 1 diagnostic confirmation):

1. **Standard English Conventions (~26% of R&W, Q15–Q21 range):** Finite, mechanical rules. Even if 3 nouns in a science sentence are unfamiliar, you can still identify the grammatical subject, check singular/plural verb agreement, and test whether two clauses are independent! Moving SEC from ~58% to **92%+** both banks fast points and saves **4–5 minutes per module** for dense reading passages.
2. **Transitions (~7–10% of R&W):** Pure logical polarity (`Support/Addition`, `Contrast/Concession`, `Cause/Effect`, `Example/Specification`). Strip Clause 1 and Clause 2 to short summaries, ignore the underlined transition, label the logical bridge, and eliminate any two transitions in the same functional family (e.g., *Furthermore* and *Moreover* cannot both be right).
3. **Rhetorical Synthesis (Bullet-Point Notes, last 2–4 Qs of each module):** Do **not** even read the bullet points until you dissect the exact goal in the question stem (*"emphasize a difference," "introduce the researcher's method to an unfamiliar audience," "illustrate a practical limitation"*). Often 2–3 choices are grammatically fine and factually true from the notes, yet fail half of the prompt's rhetorical goal.
4. **Words in Context (~15–19% of R&W, Q1–Q5):** Apply the **5-Step Context-Clue Method** so punctuation and contrast markers carry 70% of the weight before vocabulary recall even starts.
5. **Central Ideas and Details:** Anchor on the main claim + scope; eliminate choices that elevate a minor supporting example into the central thesis or introduce extreme quantifiers (*always, exclusively, never, prove conclusively*).
6. **Command of Evidence (Textual & Quantitative):** Identify the exact **two-part hypothesis** (`Variable A causes/correlates with Outcome B under Condition C`). Eliminate data choices that misread the table/graph OR report accurate numbers about the wrong variable.
7. **Inferences (Blank at the End of a Dense Paragraph):** Treat SAT inferences like strict logical deductions (`Premise 1 + Premise 2 → Necessary Conclusion`), never creative storytelling. Every word in the winning inference must be licensed by stated constraints in the passage.
8. **Text Structure & Purpose / Cross-Text Connections:** Track **structural shifts** (*Common Belief → New Challenge → Qualified Synthesis*) and map **exact point of agreement vs. disagreement** between Text 1 and Text 2 before reading choices.

> **How Grammar, Vocabulary, Structure, and Logic Interact for Non-Native Readers:**  
> Many "reading comprehension" misses by international students are actually **syntax-boundary misses**: misreading a participial modifier (*"Long dismissed by classical economists, behavioral models..."*) as the author's own rejection rather than an outdated view the author is *overturning*! Mastering **Boundaries & Modifiers** on Day 2 directly unlocks **Main Ideas, Inferences, and Text Purpose** on Days 3–5.

### B. Math: Climbing from 640 toward 700–750+

A **640 Math** baseline means your foundational arithmetic and basic algebra exist, but points leak in three places: (1) **1–3 preventable careless/misread errors in Module 1** that either cap your routing or waste cushion, (2) **medium/hard nonlinear functions, systems with constants (`no solution / infinitely many solutions`), and percent-change traps**, and (3) **slow manual algebra where Desmos solves in 15 seconds**.

- **Zero-Leak Module 1 Rule:** Treat Questions 1–15 of Module 1 with surgical verification (box the final target expression; check units/axes). Hitting `20–22 / 22` in Module 1 unlocks the full `700–800` ceiling.
- **High-Frequency Algebra & Advanced Math Mastery:** Drill linear parameters (`slope = rate of change`, `y-intercept = initial value`, parallel/perpendicular slopes, infinite/zero solutions in systems), quadratic forms (`standard`, `factored/roots`, `vertex`, `discriminant b² - 4ac`), and exponential models (`a(1 ± r)^(t/k)`).
- **Strategic Desmos Fluency:** Use Desmos instantly for systems of equations (linear or nonlinear intersections), parameter/constant problems via **sliders**, equivalent expressions (graph `f(x)` and choices to test identical curves, checking domain edge cases!), and table regressions (`y_1 ~ m x_1 + b` or `y_1 ~ a x_1^2 + b x_1 + c`). Keep crisp manual algebra for symbolic isolation ("solve for `r` in terms of `p` and `q`") and discriminant inequalities where Desmos sliders need algebraic framing first.

     - `Algebra` → `Linear equations in one variable` | `Linear functions` | `Linear equations in two variables` | `Systems of two linear equations in two variables` | `Linear inequalities in one or two variables`

### G. Digital SAT Pacing & Adaptive Module Strategy

#### 1. Reading & Writing Pacing (`32 Minutes · 27 Questions · ~71 Seconds/Question Average`)

International students lose the most points when they get bogged down on dense `Inferences` and `Command of Evidence` passages around Questions 10–14 and then rush easy `Grammar`, `Transitions`, and `Rhetorical Synthesis` points at Questions 15–27! Use the **Split-Module Two-Pass Order**:

| Pass / Question Block | Question Types | Target Time Budget | Countdown Clock Remaining | Tactical Rule |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Step 1: Jump straight to Q15 (first SEC item) → Solve Q15–Q27** | `Standard English Conventions`, `Transitions`, `Rhetorical Synthesis` (~12–13 Qs) | **10–11 minutes** (~45–50 sec/Q) | **21:00–22:00 left** | Bank mechanical grammar, transition polarity, and goal-first bullet notes while your brain is fresh! |
| **Step 2: Click Q1 → Solve Q1–Q7** | `Words in Context` + `Text Structure / Cross-Text` (~6–7 Qs) | **7–8 minutes** (~60–65 sec/Q) | **13:00–15:00 left** | Apply 5-Step Context-Clue prediction; do **not** stare at an unknown word past 65 seconds—eliminate wrong polarities, mark, and move! |
| **Step 3: Solve Q8–Q14 (Densest Reading Core)** | `Central Ideas`, `Command of Evidence`, `Inferences` (~7–8 Qs) | **11–12 minutes** (~85–95 sec/Q) | **2:00–3:00 left** | You now know *for certain* how much time you have left with Q15–27 already banked! Bracket extra clauses, match exact claim scope. |
| **Step 4: Flagged Buffer Review** | Any 2–3 marked items (never leave blanks—no guessing penalty!) | **2–3 minutes** | **0:00** | Confirm every single question `1–27` has a selected bubble by the `01:00` warning. |

**Emergency Unknown-Vocabulary Rule Under Timed Conditions:**
1. **Unfamiliar Word in the Passage:** Ignore the exact noun/adjective unless it is the core predicate; replace it mentally with `"X-thing"` or `"[positive/negative trait]"` using surrounding modifiers and keep moving.
2. **Two Unfamiliar Answer Choices in `Words in Context`:** Test the **two words you DO know** strictly against your 5-Step prediction.
   - If one known word matches both logic and tone → **Pick it confidently** (never pick an unknown word just because it "sounds fancy"!).
   - If *both* known words clearly violate your prediction → **Eliminate both immediately!** You just jumped from a 25% guess to a **50% shot**; use prefix/suffix polarity (`un-`, `in-`, `cor-`, `-ous`) to pick between the remaining two in 10 seconds, flag, and move on.
